Things that fly can hurt people when they come down. None of this is hard, but all of it matters. Reviewers will check your design and your flight video against it.

## LiPo batteries

- Charge in a LiPo bag or a metal box, on a non-flammable surface.
- Never leave a charging battery alone, and never charge overnight.
- Use a balance charger set to the right cell count.
- Puffy, dented or hot? Stop using it. Discharge it and recycle it properly.
- Store them at storage voltage (about 3.8 V per cell), not full.

## Props

- **Props off** whenever the aircraft is powered on the bench. Flashing firmware, testing motors, setting up the radio: props off.
- Keep hands, hair and sleeves out of the prop arc.
- Arm only when you're ready to fly and everyone is behind you.
- Replace cracked or chipped props. They can shatter.

## Where you fly

- Pick an open field with no one around. A local flying club field is even better.
- Check the weather. Strong wind and first flights don't mix.
- Do a range check and a control check before every flight.
- Bring a buddy to spot and to film.

## Local rules

Every country has its own rules. In the US that's the FAA; elsewhere, look up your country's aviation authority.

- Many places require you to **register anything over 250 g**.
- Most have height limits (often around 120 m / 400 ft) and keep-out zones.
- Some require a short online safety test first. In the US that's TRUST.

If you're not sure, ask in #throttle before you fly.

## Never fly over

- **People.** Not your friends, not a crowd, not "just quickly".
- **Roads** and parked cars.
- **Airports** and helipads, or anywhere near them.

## Rockets

- **Certified low-power motors only**, bought from a real supplier. No homemade motors or propellant.
- **Adult supervision** for every launch.
- Use a proper launch pad and an electrical launch system with a safety key.
- Follow the minimum distance on the motor's packaging, and launch away from buildings, trees and dry grass.
