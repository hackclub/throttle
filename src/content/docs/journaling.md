Your journal is how reviewers see your hours. A good one is quick to write and easy to trust. Write an entry at the end of every session, while you still remember what went wrong.

## What a good entry has

- **What you did.** Specific is better than long. "Cut the wing ribs and glued the spar" beats "worked on the wing".
- **What broke or surprised you.** Failed prints, wrong motor, CG way off. This is the useful part.
- **What's next.** One line is enough.
- **At least one real photo.** Your desk, your CAD screen, your half-glued fuselage. Messy is fine.
- **Honest time.** Count the time you actually worked, not the time the printer ran while you were at dinner.
- **Long sessions are fine.** Log the whole session in one entry, but say what you did across it. Reviewers look closely at long entries with little text.

## Example

> **Wing v2: ribs and spar (2 h 15 m)**
>
> Cut 14 ribs from 3 mm depron using the template from my CAD. The first four came out uneven because my blade was dull, so I swapped it and redid them. Glued the carbon spar in with foam-safe CA.
>
> The wing is about 8 g heavier than I planned. Probably the glue. Next time I'll use less and try hot glue on the leading edge.
>
> Next: cover the wing and check the CG with the battery in.
>
> *(photo: ribs laid out on the cutting mat next to the printed template)*

That's it. Nobody needs an essay.

## What gets flagged

Reviewers look at every journal before approving hours. These get hours cut or the submission returned:

- **Padded hours.** Five hours for "watched a video about props" won't fly.
- **No photos.** If there's nothing to show, it's hard to believe.
- **AI-written text or images.** Write it yourself, in your own words, with your own photos. Short and rough is fine. Generated entries can get your submission rejected.
- **Copy-paste entries.** The same text logged three times isn't three sessions.
