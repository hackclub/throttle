Throttle pays you to design something that flies, then helps you build it. You log your hours as you go, and two reviews turn those hours into money for parts and fuel for the shop.

## The flight plan

1. **Create your aircraft and pick a tier.** Give it a name, a category and a one-line pitch. Pick the tier that matches how ambitious it is (see [Tier rates](/docs/tiers)). Link the GitHub repo where your CAD, code and notes will live.
2. **Log your design hours.** Every work session gets a journal entry: what you did, a photo, and how long it took. Sketching, CAD, choosing parts, simulating, writing firmware all count. See [Good journaling](/docs/journaling).
3. **Submit for Design Review.** Once you have at least one design entry, a repo link and a `bom.csv` in the repo root, send it in. Tell us the estimated weight, battery and motors.
4. **Get your HCB grant.** A reviewer checks your journal and design. If it's approved, your design hours × your tier rate are sent to you as an HCB card for parts.
5. **Build it and log the build.** Order parts, build, test, crash, fix. Keep logging. Build hours count separately from design hours.
6. **Submit for Maiden Flight review.** Film the first real flight and submit the video with a final photo.
7. **Earn fuel and go airborne.** Approved build hours turn into fuel for the shop, and your aircraft is marked Airborne. That's a ship.

## Statuses

You'll see one of these on every aircraft:

- **Drafting**: you're designing and logging design hours.
- **Design Review**: waiting on a reviewer. Your journal is locked until it comes back.
- **Design Returned**: a reviewer wants changes. Read the feedback, fix it, resubmit.
- **Parts Funded**: your grant is approved. Go build it, and log build hours.
- **Flight Review**: your maiden flight is with a reviewer.
- **Flight Returned**: the flight needs another go, or something's missing. Check the feedback.
- **Airborne**: shipped. Fuel is on its way.
- **Rejected**: this one won't move forward. The feedback says why.
