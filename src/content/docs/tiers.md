Your tier sets how much each design hour is worth. Pick the one that matches how hard your build is, not the one that pays the most. Reviewers will check.

## The three tiers

| Tier | For | Examples | Rate |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Tier 1 | Beginner builds | Foamboard glider, simple micro-flyer | $4.50 / h |
| Tier 2 | Intermediate builds | Custom RC plane, sub-250 g quad, indoor blimp | $5.00 / h |
| Tier 3 | Advanced builds | Custom flight controller PCB, autonomous or FPV system, complex propulsion | $5.50 / h |

## Your grant

```
grant = approved design hours × your tier rate
```

There's no cap. Worked example: you log **12 h** of design work on a Tier 2 plane and it's approved.

```
12 h × $5.00 = $60.00
```

That $60.00 arrives as an HCB card you spend on parts.

## Fuel

Build hours don't add to your grant. Once your maiden flight is approved, they turn into fuel for the shop:

```
fuel = approved build hours × 10 × tier multiplier
```

| Tier | Multiplier |
| --- | --- |
| Tier 1 | 1.0 |
| Tier 2 | 1.1 |
| Tier 3 | 1.2 |

Example: 20 h of build time at Tier 3 is `20 × 10 × 1.2 = 240` fuel.

## When your tier changes

You choose a tier when you create your aircraft, and you confirm it when you submit for Design Review. The reviewer can move it up or down if the design doesn't match. Say you picked Tier 3 but the build is a foamboard glider: they'll drop it to Tier 1 and your grant is worked out at $4.50 / h.

Reviewers can also adjust approved hours if some entries look padded. Either way, the feedback tells you what changed and why. The tier set at Design Review is the one used for your fuel later.
